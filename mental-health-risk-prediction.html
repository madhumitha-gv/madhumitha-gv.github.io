Workplace Mental Health Risk Prediction

Explainable risk modeling on 1,467 survey responses

Employers catch mental health risk late, through questionnaires and interviews that miss early signals. This project models an anonymous tech-industry survey into two predictions and three interpretable workplace indices, served through an app and an API where SHAP explains every individual score.

RoleSole developer
Data1,467 responses, 63 features
ModelsXGBoost, Gradient Boosting
Served viaStreamlit and FastAPI

The problem

Mental health disorders are a growing workplace concern, but traditional detection — static questionnaires and clinical interviews — is slow and often misses early warning signs. The project set out to predict risk from survey data and to make the prediction explainable enough that HR and mental-health professionals could act on it rather than simply receive a score.

Preparing messy survey data

Survey responses are not clean categorical data. Answers like "I don't know", "N/A" and "Not sure" carry meaning that is lost if treated as missing.

  • Ambiguous responses were mapped onto explicit ordinal values rather than discarded.
  • Genuinely ordinal variables were encoded in order, so "Very easy" to "Never" keeps its ranking instead of becoming arbitrary labels.
  • Remaining categoricals were label-encoded and residual nulls imputed with column medians.

Three composite indices

Rather than feeding 63 raw columns into a model, three interpretable indices were engineered, each normalised to a 0–1 range:

IndexWhat it measures
Mental Health SupportEmployer benefits, care options, wellness programs, leave ease, formal communication
Workplace StigmaFear of consequences, observed discrimination, coworker and supervisor discomfort, career impact
Organizational OpennessParity with physical health, anonymity protection, medical coverage, interview willingness

These give a non-technical audience a high-level read on workplace culture that individual survey columns cannot.

Models and results

Two separate models, each trained on the ten features most correlated with its own target:

ModelAlgorithmAccuracyF1
Current mental health disorderXGBoost~82%~0.79
Likelihood of seeking treatmentGradient Boosting~85%~0.83

Each outputs a risk level, a probability, and SHAP-based feature importance. SHAP waterfall plots show which features drove an individual prediction, which is what turns a risk score into something a professional can question rather than simply trust.

Delivered two ways

  • Streamlit app: a 15-question survey, real-time colour-coded risk gauges, a SHAP explanation tab, prioritised recommendations and an analytics tab with benchmarks.
  • FastAPI backend: /predict for a single employee, /batch-predict with summary statistics, plus /health, /metrics and /train for retraining from an uploaded CSV, with auto-generated docs.

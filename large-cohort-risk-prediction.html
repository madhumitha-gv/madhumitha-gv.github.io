Large-Cohort Risk Prediction

Predictive modeling and statistical evaluation, Indiana University

Building a 200,000-participant analytical cohort in SQL and a PCA and XGBoost pipeline on top of it, evaluated for calibration and fairness rather than headline accuracy alone.

RoleData scientist, predictive analytics
OrganizationIndiana University
TimelineJune 2025 - May 2026
Headline0.802 AUROC

The problem

Predict an outcome across a large population from many overlapping attributes, and do it in a way somebody would be willing to act on. With a rare outcome, a model can look excellent on accuracy while being useless in practice, so the work had to be trustworthy and explainable, not just performant.

The data

  • 200,000+ participants, assembled in SQL.
  • Demographics, medications, comorbidities and clinical features integrated into a single analytical table.

Approach

  1. Cohort construction. Wrote the SQL defining the cohort and joining every feature source into one table.
  2. Dimensionality reduction. Used PCA to compress heavily correlated features.
  3. Modeling. Trained XGBoost under 5-fold cross-validation.
  4. Evaluation. Led with PR-AUC rather than AUROC, since the positive class is rare and AUROC flatters a model in that setting. Checked calibration, ran ablation studies and subgroup analysis, and used statistical testing to confirm differences were real.
  5. Communication. Turned the evaluation into findings and recommendations the research team could act on.

Results

MeasureResult
AUROC0.802
PR-AUC0.379
Validation5-fold cross-validation
Cohort size200,000+

What I took from it

  • PR-AUC is the honest metric when positives are rare. Reporting AUROC alone would have made this model look considerably better than it is.
  • Ablation studies showed which feature groups genuinely earn their place, and several did not.

What I would do next

  • Validate against an external cohort, or one drawn from a later time period.
  • Add SHAP explanations at the level of individual predictions, not just global importance.
